# idk what i am calling this

(This README is a quick overview of this repo. If you are overwhelmed by all the words and don't know where to start, look at the`aaaaa-help` folder! )

Computational researchers have to know a lot of meta-skills that don't ever get taught formally: project management, using a shell, code organization, writing, figure making, etc. I was really overwhelmed when I first started. I desperately wanted one place where I could figure out what I needed to learn and find resources for learning them. As I've gained more experience doing research and continuing to learn new things, I've also realized that documenting things makes it way easier to work with other people, including your past/future self. 

Because I am benevolent and also forget anything I haven't used in the past month, I made this repo to deposit all the knowledge I have hunted and gathered. Hopefully this will allow others to not suffer as I have. It is very much under construction at the moment, but it appears that it will become a conglomeration of explanatory guides for the fundamentals of meta-skills, summaries of advanced/niche technical skills, opinionated standard procedures for doing things (e.g., writing and figure making), and reference sheets for when you forget a shortcut or command.

Three caveats: 
1. This is by no means meant to be an exhaustive list, and I do not aspire to replicate all relevant knowledge that exists. The documents here are meant more as guides than exhaustive manuals. I reference more in-depth resources and actual manuals heavily.
2. The specific apps/workflows/conventions/shortcuts listed are what works for me, but are by no means the optimal choice for everyone. If you like other things, you can fork this repo and keep what you want! If you think there's a big gap or I'm using something very obviously inferior, tell me (or open an issue, or better yet, dump your knowledge and make a pull request)!
3. I enjoy doing the intellectual labor of research and I don't think conceptual understanding is cleanly separable from low-level implementation. Especially for things you are new to, actually doing things, experiencing failure, and gradually building up your knowledge is a long-term investment in your competence (and also rewarding). So for most topics, there's a conspicuous lack of information on how to integrate LLMs and agents (the few exceptions are for basic copy editing and as a search engine, where natural language understanding is very helpful). Many rote tasks are automatable in deterministic way. If you want to learn to use agents and outsource your intellectual labor, you should look elsewhere \derogatory.

The basic organization of the repo is as follows: Each subfolder also has its own README with more details!
- `aaaaa-help`: how to use this repo, recommended sequences for acquiring skills, actionable advice for practicing and improving
- `cheatsheets`: quick reference sheets on syntax/commands for commonly used tools and non-coding coding languages. These are super utilitarian so there's very minimal explanations.
- `code-management`: guides for coding-related meta-skills, and standard procedures for organizing and maintaining code. 
- `command-line`: fundamentals for the command-line interface and guides/notes on specific shells or CLI tools.
- `shortcuts-and-snippets`: lists of keybinds, macros, and snippets for apps and editors.
- `writing`: protocols and didactic guides for writing and making figures about your work, relevant style guides, and links to good examples of scientific writing.
